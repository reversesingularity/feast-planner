Project Blueprint: The Feast Planner Web Application
I. Application Blueprint: User Experience and Feature Architecture
This section delineates the functional architecture and user experience (UX) design for the Feast Planner application. The blueprint is derived from an analysis of best-in-class travel and financial planning applications, adapted to serve the specific, nuanced requirements of attendees of the annual Feast of Tabernacles hosted by various Church of God organizations. The application is envisioned as an indispensable digital companion for the entire lifecycle of the Feast journey, from initial planning and budgeting to on-site navigation and expense management.
A. The Unified Itinerary Dashboard
The core of the user experience will be a centralized dashboard that consolidates all trip-related information into a single, chronological master itinerary. This concept is directly inspired by the primary value proposition of applications like TripIt, which excel at organizing disparate travel confirmations into a coherent timeline. For the Feast Planner, this dashboard becomes the user's single source of truth for their entire trip.
The dashboard will be composed of several key components. A master itinerary view will present a timeline of all trip elements, including flights, hotel reservations, car rentals, and user-added custom events. Crucially, this is not merely a passive display of user-entered data. A key differentiator is the direct integration of official Feast site information. When a user selects a destination Feast site from the application's database, the itinerary will be automatically populated with the official schedule of daily church services, seminars, family dances, youth programs, and other planned activities as announced by the host church. This transforms the application from a generic planner into an essential, context-aware event guide.
The observation that the Feast of Tabernacles is a highly structured, family-focused event with a distinct spiritual purpose, rather than a casual vacation, is foundational to this design. The research details specific events such as "family dance event," "seniors' luncheon," "teen program, Ignite," and "children's classes... Sparks". This necessitates a feature set that moves beyond generic travel planning to cater specifically to the activities of the Feast. This directly impacts the application's architecture; the itinerary must be capable of ingesting and displaying a pre-defined, official schedule. This functionality establishes a significant value proposition, making the application an indispensable tool for attendees by serving as the de facto program guide for the event.
For the Minimum Viable Product (MVP), data entry will be facilitated through structured manual entry forms. These forms will be optimized for rapid input of travel details such as confirmation numbers, dates, times, and addresses, providing a robust alternative before the implementation of more advanced features like automated email parsing.
Adhering to mobile-first principles, the itinerary must be fully accessible offline. This is a critical requirement, as attendees may experience limited or unreliable internet connectivity at various Feast sites, which are often in resort or rural locations. Finally, to ensure seamless integration with the user's broader digital life, the application will support synchronization of the trip itinerary with personal calendar services like Google Calendar and Apple's iCal, a standard feature in modern planning tools.
B. The Financial Command Center
A dedicated financial module will provide users with comprehensive tools for budget planning, expense tracking, and group financial management, specifically tailored to the context of attending the Feast of Tabernacles. This module synthesizes the proactive, envelope-based budgeting philosophy of applications like You Need a Budget (YNAB) with the collaborative expense-splitting capabilities of platforms like Splitwise.
The module's foundation is a pre-trip budget planner. Users will be able to construct a detailed budget using a set of customizable categories. To streamline this process, the application will offer a pre-populated template based on typical Feast-related expenses, such as Tithe, Offerings, Lodging, Transportation, Food, Family Activities, and Souvenirs. This feature embodies the YNAB principle of "giving every dollar a job," encouraging thoughtful financial planning well before the trip commences.
During the Feast, a real-time expense tracker will allow users to log expenditures on the go. Each expense can be assigned to a pre-defined budget category, and the application will provide immediate visual feedback on spending against the budget through progress bars and notifications. This helps users stay on track and avoid overspending.
Recognizing that many attendees travel in families or groups, a key feature will be a group expense splitting tool, provisionally named "The Tabernacle Tab." Inspired directly by Splitwise's functionality, this tool will allow a designated group member to log a shared expense, such as a restaurant bill or grocery run, and split it among participants. The split can be equal or based on specific amounts. The application will maintain a running, simplified balance of who owes whom, making settlement straightforward at any point during or after the trip.
Given the global distribution of Feast sites across numerous countries, multi-currency support is an essential requirement. The expense tracker will allow users to log expenses in any local currency. The system will then use a real-time exchange rate API to convert these amounts back to the user's designated home currency, ensuring all budget tracking and reporting remains consistent and easy to understand.
C. The Global Feast Site Discovery Engine
The cornerstone of the Feast Planner application is its discovery engine: a comprehensive, searchable, and filterable database of all known Feast of Tabernacles sites across the spectrum of major Church of God organizations. This feature functions as a highly specialized version of discovery platforms like TripAdvisor, tailored to the unique needs of the Church of God community.
The user interface for discovery will be centered around an interactive global map, which will display pins for all available Feast sites, allowing for intuitive geographical exploration. Complementing the map will be a powerful search and filtering system. Users will be able to narrow down the list of sites by year, specific Church of God organization, country, and region. Furthermore, they can apply attribute-based filters to find sites that meet specific criteria, such as those offering "Family-friendly activities," "On-site housing," or a "Live webcast" for remote participation.
Each pin on the map or entry in the search results will link to a detailed site page. These pages will serve as a centralized repository of aggregated information, collating data that is often scattered across various church websites and announcements. The content of each site page will include:
 * Logistical Information: The official name of the Feast site, the venue's name and physical address, the festival dates, and the name of the hosting Church of God organization.
 * Schedules and Activities: A detailed schedule of daily worship services, planned seminars, Bible studies, and special activities like family dances or youth events.
 * Accommodation Details: Information on official hotel blocks, including negotiated group rates, specific booking codes or links, reservation deadlines, and contact numbers for reservations.
 * Local Area Information: Curated details about nearby attractions, recommended restaurants, and other points of interest that are often mentioned in church announcements to enhance the Feast experience for attendees.
 * Official Contacts: The names, phone numbers, and email addresses of the designated Feast coordinators or hosts for attendee assistance.
D. Community and Collaboration Features
To support the inherently collaborative nature of planning and attending the Feast, the application will incorporate features designed to facilitate communication and coordination among travelers.
The primary collaboration tool will be itinerary sharing. A user can share their complete trip itinerary with family members or other travel companions via a secure, private link. This functionality, modeled after TripIt's "Inner Circle" feature, allows for different levels of access; recipients can be granted either view-only or full edit permissions, ensuring that everyone in the group is synchronized.
Additionally, the application will provide a secure document storage vault. This feature allows users to upload and store digital copies of essential travel documents, such as flight and hotel confirmation PDFs, scanned passport pages, or QR codes for event tickets. This centralizes critical information and ensures it is readily accessible within the app, even when offline.
As a future enhancement in Phase 3 of the development roadmap, the platform could be expanded to include user-generated content. This would allow attendees to submit their own reviews, photos, and practical tips for specific Feast sites, creating a rich, community-driven knowledge base that would further enhance the value of the discovery engine, much like the user-generated content model of TripAdvisor.

II. Technical Architecture and Full-Stack Technology Selection
This section specifies the underlying technology stack and architectural patterns that will be employed to build the Feast Planner application. The technical decisions outlined herein are guided by the project's core requirements: superior performance on mobile devices, cost-effective scalability, robust security, and seamless integration with the specified AI-driven development workflow.
A. Front-End Framework Recommendation: Svelte
The choice of a front-end JavaScript framework is a critical architectural decision. While React stands as the industry incumbent with an unparalleled ecosystem, and Vue is lauded for its gentle learning curve, Svelte presents a compelling set of advantages that align exceptionally well with the specific goals of this project. Therefore, Svelte is the recommended framework for the Feast Planner's front-end.
This recommendation is based on three primary factors:
 * Performance and Bundle Size: Svelte operates as a compiler, shifting the bulk of the work from the user's browser at runtime to the development machine at build time. This process generates highly optimized, minimal vanilla JavaScript, resulting in significantly smaller application bundle sizes and faster initial load times and runtime performance. This performance-first approach is paramount for a mobile-first application, where users may be accessing the app over slower or less reliable mobile networks while traveling.
 * Developer Experience and Productivity: Svelte's syntax is intentionally simple and closely mirrors standard HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. This reduces the amount of boilerplate code and lowers the cognitive overhead for developers, enabling faster development cycles. This simplicity is particularly advantageous when working with AI code generation tools, as it allows them to produce cleaner, more direct, and more easily verifiable code.
 * Modernity and Future-Proofing: While its ecosystem is more focused than React's, Svelte is a mature and rapidly growing framework. The accompanying application framework, SvelteKit, provides a comprehensive, production-ready solution for routing, server-side rendering, and other advanced application needs. For a new project commencing in 2025, selecting Svelte represents a forward-looking architectural choice that prioritizes end-user performance and developer efficiency.
For styling, the application will utilize Tailwind CSS. This utility-first CSS framework is highly compatible with component-based architectures like Svelte. Its methodology involves applying small, single-purpose utility classes directly within the HTML markup. This approach provides maximum design flexibility and avoids the generic appearance often associated with pre-built component libraries like Bootstrap, while being less opinionated and more customizable than systems like Material-UI.
B. Back-End Architecture: A Serverless Approach with AWS Lambda
A serverless architecture is the optimal choice for the application's back-end, given its anticipated usage patterns. The traffic is expected to be highly seasonal, with significant peaks during the months leading up to and during the annual Feast of Tabernacles, followed by periods of lower activity.
AWS Lambda is the selected compute service. Lambda allows for the execution of code in response to triggers without the need to provision or manage servers. Billing is based on the number of requests and the duration of code execution, measured in milliseconds. This pay-per-use model is exceptionally cost-effective for applications with variable or intermittent traffic, eliminating the costs associated with idle server capacity.
The back-end will be structured as a set of microservices, each implemented as a collection of Lambda functions:
 * auth-service: A set of functions to handle user registration, login, password management, and the issuance and validation of JWTs.
 * trip-service: Functions providing CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) operations for user trips, itineraries, and individual itinerary items.
 * feast-site-service: Functions dedicated to serving the aggregated Feast site data to the front-end, including search and filtering logic.
 * expense-service: Functions for managing budgets and tracking expenses, including logic for group expense calculations.
 * data-aggregator-service: A scheduled Lambda function, triggered by Amazon EventBridge, that will periodically execute web scraping or data fetching tasks to keep the Feast site database current.
This serverless architecture will be supported by several other key AWS services:
 * Amazon API Gateway: This service will act as the front door for all application data requests, managing RESTful API endpoints that securely trigger the appropriate Lambda functions.
 * Amazon DynamoDB: A fully managed NoSQL database service that provides the fast performance, seamless scalability, and flexible data model required for this application.
 * Amazon S3 (Simple Storage Service): Used for secure storage of user-uploaded assets, such as travel document scans, and for hosting static front-end assets.
 * Amazon Cognito: A comprehensive user identity and access management service that will handle user authentication, authorization, and user pool management, integrating seamlessly with API Gateway and Lambda to secure the application's APIs.
C. Database Design and Data Modeling (DynamoDB)
The selection of a NoSQL database, specifically Amazon DynamoDB, is a strategic architectural decision driven directly by the primary data challenge of this project: the inconsistent and heterogeneous nature of the source data from various Church of God websites. A review of these sources reveals a complete lack of a standardized format for announcing Feast site information. Attempting to model this data in a rigid SQL schema with a fixed set of columns would be impractical and brittle, requiring constant schema alterations.
A NoSQL schema, by contrast, offers the flexibility to store diverse data structures for each record. This allows the system to capture rich, detailed information for a site when it is available, and sparse information when it is not, without compromising the integrity of the database. This approach enables a core architectural principle for the application: graceful degradation of data. The front-end will be designed to elegantly handle missing data points, for instance, by simply not rendering an "Activities" section if that data is not present for a particular Feast site, rather than displaying empty fields or generating errors.

D. User Authentication and Security Protocol
The application will implement a robust and secure authentication flow based on the industry-standard OAuth 2.0 protocol, utilizing JSON Web Tokens (JWT) for session management. This approach provides a secure, stateless, and scalable method for handling user identity.
The authentication workflow will proceed as follows:
 * User Authentication: A user will register or log in via Amazon Cognito, either with an email and password or through a federated identity provider such as Google or Apple.
 * Token Issuance: Upon successful authentication, the back-end (Cognito) will generate two tokens: a short-lived Access Token (JWT), which grants access to protected API resources, and a long-lived Refresh Token.
 * Secure Token Storage: These tokens will be stored securely on the client. For a web application, this will be accomplished using HttpOnly, Secure, and SameSite=Strict cookies. This is a critical security measure that prevents the tokens from being accessed by client-side JavaScript, thereby mitigating the risk of Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) attacks.
 * Authenticated API Requests: For every subsequent request to a protected API endpoint, the client will automatically include the Access Token (JWT) in the Authorization: Bearer <token> header. The Amazon API Gateway will be configured to validate the token's signature before forwarding the request to the corresponding Lambda function.
 * Automatic Token Refresh: The Access Token is intentionally short-lived (e.g., 15 minutes) to limit the window of opportunity should it be compromised. When it expires, the front-end application will use an API interceptor to automatically and silently use the long-lived Refresh Token to obtain a new Access Token from Cognito, ensuring a seamless user experience without requiring a re-login.
 * Logout and Revocation: When a user logs out, the tokens are cleared from the client's storage. The associated Refresh Token is then explicitly revoked on the server-side to invalidate the session completely.
This entire process will be governed by strict security best practices, including the enforcement of HTTPS for all communications, secure server-side storage of all client credentials, and the implementation of rate limiting on authentication endpoints to defend against brute-force attacks.

 * Maintaining Context: To ensure Copilot generates the most accurate and relevant suggestions, developers will maintain a clean workspace, keeping related files open to provide the AI with the necessary context. IV. Mobile-First Design and Implementation Strategy
The mandate for a "mobile-first" application is more than a technical checkbox; it is a design philosophy that will guide the entire development process. This approach prioritizes the user experience on smaller screens, which not only serves the majority of modern web traffic but also leads to a cleaner, more focused, and higher-performing application across all devices.
A. Core Principles of Mobile-First Design
The implementation will be guided by three core principles:
 * Content Prioritization: The design process will begin with the smallest viewport (a typical smartphone screen). This constraint forces a rigorous prioritization of content and functionality, ensuring that only the most essential elements are presented to the user. For the Feast Planner, this means the mobile dashboard will prominently feature the next upcoming event in the user's itinerary and a quick-access button for adding a new expense. This "content-first" methodology results in a user experience that is inherently clear and uncluttered.
 * Progressive Enhancement: The application will be built upon a baseline experience that is functional and accessible on all devices. From this foundation, more complex features, larger graphical assets, and more sophisticated layouts will be progressively added for devices with larger screens, such as tablets and desktops. For example, the desktop dashboard might feature a multi-column layout displaying the itinerary alongside an interactive map of the Feast site, an enhancement that takes advantage of the additional screen real estate. This approach contrasts with "graceful degradation," where a complex desktop site is stripped of features for mobile, often resulting in a compromised experience.
 * Performance Optimization: A mobile-first strategy naturally leads to better performance. By starting with the constraints of mobile networks and devices, developers are encouraged from the outset to use optimized images, write minimal and efficient code, and employ smart loading strategies, which benefits users on all platforms.
The conscious decision to design for mobile first, with its inherent constraints, forces a level of discipline that ultimately benefits all users. It compels a deep analysis of user goals, leading to a more intuitive and less overwhelming experience on every device. This demonstrates that design constraints, when embraced, can foster superior product design.
B. Component Design for a Responsive Experience with Tailwind CSS
The practical implementation of the mobile-first philosophy will be evident in the design of every UI component, built responsively using Tailwind CSS.
 * Touch-Friendly Interactions: All interactive elements, including buttons, links, and form fields, will be designed for easy and accurate tapping. A minimum touch target size of 44x44 pixels will be enforced, with sufficient margin between elements to prevent frustrating "fat-finger" errors.
 * Responsive Typography and Layouts: A fluid typographic scale using relative units (rem) will ensure text remains legible across all screen sizes, with a minimum body text size of 16px. Layouts will be constructed using flexible grid systems (CSS Flexbox and Grid) and relative width units (%, vw), allowing the UI to adapt fluidly to any viewport. Images will be configured to scale within their containers to prevent them from breaking the layout on smaller screens.
 * Adaptive Navigation: The application's navigation will adapt to the available screen space. On mobile devices, a "hamburger" menu icon will conceal the main navigation links, preserving screen real estate for primary content. Key, frequently used actions may be placed in a bottom tab bar for easy one-handed access. On desktop screens, this menu will expand into a traditional, always-visible header navigation bar.

V. Data Aggregation and Third-Party API Integration

A. Feast Site Data Management Strategy
The central challenge and primary value proposition of the application is the aggregation of Feast of Tabernacles site information from a multitude of independent Church of God organizations. These organizations publish their data on their own schedules, using disparate formats across their respective websites, with no central repository or API available.
The initial data management strategy will be a pragmatic, multi-pronged approach:
 * Source Identification and Cataloging: The first step is to conduct ongoing research to identify and catalog all relevant Church of God organizations and the specific URLs where they publish their annual Feast information. This information will be maintained in a living document that guides all data aggregation efforts.
 * Manual & Semi-Automated Aggregation (MVP): For the initial launch, data will be populated through a combination of manual entry and targeted scraping. An administrator will manually input the foundational data for each site. Concurrently, simple web scraping scripts (developed in Node.js or Python) will be deployed as scheduled AWS Lambda functions. These scripts will monitor the cataloged URLs for changes, flagging new or updated information for administrative review and entry. This semi-automated approach provides a balance between accuracy and efficiency for the MVP.
 * Community Sourcing (Future Phase): In a later phase, a feature can be introduced to leverage the user community. Trusted users or designated "Site Ambassadors" could be granted permissions to submit new site information or suggest updates to existing data. All community-sourced data would enter a moderation queue for administrative review and approval before being published, ensuring data integrity.

VI. Phased Development and Deployment Roadmap
A phased development and deployment strategy will be employed to deliver value to users as quickly as possible while allowing for iterative refinement and the addition of more complex features over time. This approach mitigates risk and allows user feedback from early phases to inform the development of later ones.
A. Phase 1: Core Functionality and Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
The primary goal of Phase 1 is to launch a functional and highly valuable application that addresses the most critical user need: discovering Feast sites and organizing a basic plan to attend one. This MVP will establish the application's core value proposition and begin building a user base.
 * Key Features for MVP:
   * Secure User Authentication: Including registration, login, and basic profile management.
   * Global Feast Site Discovery Engine: The core feature, including the interactive map, search and filtering capabilities, and detailed site pages populated with manually aggregated data.
   * Manual Itinerary Creation: The ability for users to manually add their own travel plans, such as flights, hotel bookings, and car rentals.
   * Automatic Schedule Population: When a user selects a Feast site for their trip, the application will automatically populate their itinerary with the official schedule of services and major activities for that site.
   * Basic Offline Access: The user's current trip itinerary will be cached on their device for offline viewing.
 * Estimated Timeline: 3-4 Months.
B. Phase 2: Financial Module and Collaboration
With the core planning functionality in place, Phase 2 will focus on introducing the comprehensive financial management and group collaboration tools, significantly expanding the application's utility.
 * Key Features for Phase 2:
   * Pre-Trip Budget Planner: The full budgeting module, allowing users to set spending goals across customizable categories.
   * Real-Time Expense Tracker: The ability to log expenses on the go, with multi-currency support powered by an integrated API.
   * Group Expense Splitting: The "Tabernacle Tab" feature for managing and simplifying shared expenses among travel companions.
   * Itinerary Sharing: Functionality for users to share their trip plans with others, with options for view-only or edit permissions.
   * Secure Document Upload: A vault for users to store digital copies of important travel documents.
 * Estimated Timeline: 2-3 Months following the completion of Phase 1.
C. Phase 3: Advanced Features and Scalability
Phase 3 is focused on long-term growth, introducing advanced automation, deeper third-party integrations, and community-driven features that will position the Feast Planner as the definitive, market-leading tool for the Church of God community.
 * Key Features for Phase 3:
   * Automated Itinerary Parsing: Development of a system that allows users to forward travel confirmation emails (from airlines, hotels, etc.) to a specific address, which will then automatically parse the details and add them to the user's itinerary, mirroring a key feature of TripIt.
   * Third-Party Booking Integrations: Integrating APIs for flights and hotels to allow users to search for and book travel directly within the application.
   * User-Generated Content: Implementing features for users to submit reviews, ratings, photos, and practical tips for Feast sites, building a valuable community knowledge base.
   * Advanced Notifications: A system for sending push notifications for important events, such as flight check-in reminders, gate changes, or updates to the Feast activity schedule, similar to the premium features of TripIt Pro.
   * Enhanced Data Aggregation: Evolving the data collection process with more sophisticated scraping techniques and potentially establishing data-sharing partnerships with willing Church of God organizations.
 * Estimated Timeline: Ongoing and iterative, commencing after the successful launch of Phase 2.

Successful execution of this blueprint will result in a polished, professional, and highly functional application that directly addresses the specific needs of its target audience, simplifying the logistical and financial complexities of attending the Feast of Tabernacles and allowing attendees to better focus on the spiritual purpose of the event.
